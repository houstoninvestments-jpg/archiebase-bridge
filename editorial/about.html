A little about the person behind the site

My first business had two wheels.And a lawn mower.

I was eleven. I towed the mower behind my bike around the neighborhood, trying to build something of my own. Small business was already part of my world: I grew up around a family gutter installation business and worked alongside my dad. I loved making things work, learning as I went and seeing what I could figure out.

I grew up in small business

Before I knew anything about AI, I knew what it took to run a business.

Small business has always been part of my life. My family had a gutter installation business, and I grew up working alongside my dad, seeing construction up close and learning what it takes to make a living doing real work. A lot of my relatives built their own businesses too, from finance and technology to mom-and-pop restaurants and plenty in between. I grew up around people who made their own way, solved problems as they came and kept showing up.

I've spent long days under sinks with water dripping on me. Pulled old roofs off, put new ones on, climbed high up on apartment buildings, driven thousands of screws into decks, and helped build custom homes, yurts and skateparks here and in other countries. I've spent a lot of time learning how to make something real with my hands.

I've made more drinks than I could count from behind a bar, listened to thousands of conversations, and worked around point-of-sale systems that were supposed to help people move faster but sometimes did the opposite. I've gone door to door and taken more than ten thousand rides and delivery trips. You notice a lot when you're paying attention.

I've also built and tried online businesses, including websites, blogs and Amazon FBA, and chased new ideas through design, software and now AI. Some worked. Some didn't. Every one taught me something about how a real person experiences the thing you're trying to build.

I've traveled a lot, been in places where businesses run very differently, and met people from every walk of life. Over time, I realized the same habit followed me everywhere. I notice where people get stuck. The extra step. The confusing message. The pattern nobody has stopped to question. And I know that behind every business system are real people trying to get through their day.

I don't think of myself as the expert in everything. I'm a wide-ranging builder who loves getting curious, zooming out and figuring out what the problem really is before choosing a solution. I keep learning, and when the right answer needs a specialist, I'm comfortable saying that.

A few things the work taught me

Real life has a way of exposing what doesn't work.

01 / UNDER THE SINK

The fix you can reach matters.

A brilliant solution isn't worth much when the person doing the job can't actually use it.

02 / BEHIND THE BAR

Every extra click matters.

When a place gets busy, you learn quickly which systems help and which ones everyone works around.

03 / ON THE ROAD

The details change everything.

Real people don't behave like flowcharts. Clear instructions and thoughtful little decisions make a difference.

04 / BEHIND THE SCREEN

Build for the person, not the demo.

A website, tool or automation only succeeds if it actually makes something better for someone.
