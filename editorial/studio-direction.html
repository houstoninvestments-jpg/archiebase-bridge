A SMALL-BUSINESS STUDIO / BUILT AROUND REAL LIFE

You built a business to have a life.Somewhere along the way, it started taking over.

The dream was freedom. Then came the phone calls you couldn't answer, quotes you forgot to chase, late-night invoices, and little fires that never seem to end. Even when business is good, it's exhausting to feel like you can never quite catch up.

But maybe time isn't your problem. Maybe the website doesn't represent the business you've built. Your staff is fighting the same clunky system every day. Or you've got an idea that could make things better, and no clue where to begin.

I'm Aaron. I love understanding what's really going on, noticing the things other people have learned to live with, and finding the smartest, most practical way to make them better. Sometimes that's design. Sometimes it's AI or automation. Sometimes it's one simple change nobody thought to try.

THE STUFF THAT GETS UNDER YOUR SKIN

Most business problems don't announce themselves.

They're the missed opportunity, the thing you've been meaning to fix for six months, or the same ten-minute chore that somehow steals another evening. I want to see what's actually happening before suggesting anything.

01

"It's 9:30. Did I ever follow up with that quote?"

You did the site visit. Figured out the numbers. Sent the estimate. Then the day swallowed you whole. Now you're at home still trying to remember what got missed.

The fix might be surprisingly small.

An approved follow-up and one reliable reminder could keep the conversation moving without adding another spreadsheet to your life.

Fewer loose ends. A quieter mind after work.

02

"Our work is excellent. Our website doesn't show it."

You've earned your reputation the hard way. But a stranger lands on the site and sees generic words, confusing choices or pictures that don't feel anything like you.

Maybe your business needs a better first impression.

Better story, sharper design, clearer offers, easier contact. Not a shiny rebuild just because someone wants to sell you one.

More trust. Clearer communication. A site worth sending people to.

03

"I can't answer the phone with both hands full."

You're up a ladder, under a sink or helping somebody face to face. The phone rings. By the time you call back, they may already be talking to someone else.

Your customers can hear back without pulling you off the job.

Sometimes an automatic first text that asks what they need is enough to keep that opportunity open until you can respond.

Fewer customers left waiting. Less phone anxiety.

In CallRail's data, roughly one in four inbound calls went unanswered. Call data

04

"Why are we typing the same thing into three different places?"

You're paying for software, but your team has workarounds for the workarounds. Nobody has time to trace all the steps. It just becomes how things are done.

That's the kind of friction I love finding.

Sometimes the fix is connecting two tools. Sometimes it's removing a step entirely. I care about what happens on a busy Tuesday, not what a demo says should happen.

Less rework. Fewer mistakes. A smoother day.

05

"Business is fine. I just have this idea..."

Maybe you've imagined a better customer experience, a tool for your team or a totally different way to show your work. You don't have to be struggling to want something better.

Good ideas shouldn't have to stay ideas.

We can pull it apart, build a small useful version and see what it actually does. A healthy business deserves creativity too.

Something real to try, not another project that lives in your head.

Notice something? These don't start with AI, SEO or an app. They start with what people actually experience.

HOW MY MIND WORKS

Zoom out. Look closer. Then build.

I'm wired to notice little patterns, subtle friction and the things people learn to work around. I get more excited about discovering the real problem than pitching the latest tool.

WHY I SEE THINGS THE WAY I DO

I didn't learn business from an AI course.I grew up in it.

My family had a gutter installation business. I worked alongside my dad and saw the ins and outs of building something with your hands while keeping customers, jobs and money moving. Relatives of mine have run everything from finance and tech businesses to mom-and-pop restaurants. Small business was the world I grew up around.

I've crawled under countless sinks getting dripped on. Worked up high on apartment buildings, torn off roofs, put them back on, driven thousands of screws into decks, helped build homes, yurts and skateparks here and abroad. I've spent long shifts behind bars, worked with all kinds of point-of-sale systems, gone door to door and driven more than ten thousand rides and deliveries.

I've also built websites, tried online businesses, worked with creative tools and fallen deep into what AI can make possible. I've spent years watching people, asking questions, and noticing the tiny details that make something easier, harder, clearer or just plain frustrating.

That's why I don't see a business as a list of software products. I see the people, the work and all the little connections between them. I like figuring out which change will matter most, and I keep at it until I find a sensible way forward.

I'm not pretending to be an expert in everything. I know where my strengths are, and when a specialist would be better. But my range helps me look at a problem from directions a one-service shop might miss.

Archiebase is the name. I'm the person you talk to and the person who works on the solution.

THE PART NOBODY PUTS IN A PROFIT & LOSS REPORT

Sometimes you get home.But the business comes home with you.

It's 9:30 at night and you're thinking about the quote you forgot. You're sitting next to someone you love, but half your mind is still at work. You started this whole thing hoping for more freedom, and some days it feels like the business owns all of it.

Maybe you get back to the work you love. Maybe you get back to the people you love. Either way, it's worth fixing what's taking more of you than it should.

"Are you going to make it to my game?"You want to say yes without wondering what's going to fall apart while you're gone.

"Can you put your phone down for a minute?"You're physically home. Mentally you're still catching up on yesterday.

"We should actually take a weekend off."You'd love to. You're already worrying about Monday.
